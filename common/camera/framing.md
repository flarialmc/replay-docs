# Framing and Field of View

Good camera path can still look bad if subject is hard to see.

## Subject placement

### Center

Clear and stable. Good for tutorials, reveals, and exact comparison.

### Rule of thirds

Place subject near one-third lines for more space and direction.

### Lead room

Leave space in front of moving subject. Viewer sees where action goes.

### Headroom

Keep small space above player/entity. Too much makes subject feel low; too little feels cramped.

## Horizon

Keep horizon level unless tilt has purpose. Slight accidental roll looks like error.

## Field of view (FOV)

- **Wide FOV:** shows more world, increases sense of speed, exaggerates edges.
- **Narrow FOV:** isolates subject, compresses distance, makes movement calmer.

Changing camera distance and FOV are not same. Moving closer changes perspective; narrowing FOV mostly changes framing.

## FOV animation

Small slow change can support reveal. Fast large change feels like zoom effect and may distract.

Start/end FOV keys should use easing. Avoid changing FOV while camera also makes complex turn unless tested carefully.

## Camera height

- Low angle: subject feels large/powerful
- Eye level: neutral/readable
- High angle: shows layout/context
- Top-down: strategy/world overview

## Preview checklist

- Main subject readable?
- Important action inside frame throughout?
- HUD hidden if unwanted?
- Camera enters blocks?
- FOV comfortable?
- Horizon intentional?
